# Engineer

One sales engineer's own book: product lines, monthly run, targets, unbilled projects, and the customers who owe them. No screenshot is included in this guide; the page shares its table and strip conventions with the vertical detail page.

## What is on the page

- A six-figure strip: YTD revenue, YTD revenue less budget, full-year forecast less budget, return on investment year to date (with year-to-date cost to employ named beside it), direct contribution year to date (gross margin less cost to employ, before vertical and group costs), and net to collect.
- **Sales performance by product line**: a monthly chart, then a table of this engineer's own product lines, with a total row.
- **Business targets by product line**: the same shape used on the vertical page, for this engineer's own products.
- **Unbilled projects**: a narrower version of the vertical page's table (no engineer column, since it is implicit, and only the aggregate aged-over-60-days figure rather than the full eight bands), followed by the same five-step month bridge component the vertical page uses.
- **Receivables by customer**: one row per customer this engineer is responsible for, plus this engineer's own reasons breakdown.

This page deliberately carries no profit and loss section. Costs other than cost to employ are booked at the vertical level, not per person, so a profit and loss per engineer would misattribute vertical and group costs; the page states this exclusion in words rather than silently omitting the section.

## How the figures are built

An engineer's page always resolves to their own home vertical: opening an engineer's URL under any other vertical redirects to their canonical address, so a link can never point at a stale or wrong vertical for that person. Return on investment is computed per engineer, not per product line, which is why the total row on the product-line table is the only row carrying an ROI figure.

## Interactions

A row of links to peer engineers in the same vertical sits beside the page heading, plus a link back to that vertical's full sheet.
